# National Park Free Entrance Days (2026)

## What it is

Every year the **National Park Service** waives entrance fees at all NPS sites on a set of **fee-free days**. More than 300 of the ~430 NPS units have no entrance fee at all, all year — the fee-free days apply to the parks that do charge (national parks like Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Yosemite, Acadia, and the 100+ others with entrance fees).

> **New for 2026:** NPS fee-free days are for **U.S. residents only**. Non-resident visitors without an annual pass face an added $100-per-person surcharge at the 11 most-visited national parks, on top of standard fees.

Published by the National Park Service (source: <https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/fee-free-parks.htm>):

## What's waived and what isn't

- **Waived:** standard entrance fees at all fee-charging NPS sites (including commercial tour and transportation entrance fees at many parks).
- **Not waived:** camping fees, timed-entry reservation fees (some of these parks — Arches, Zion, Glacier — require reservations separate from entrance fees), special-use permits, and fees collected by concessionaires for lodging, boat launches, tours, and parking.

## Free days-*also* on other federal lands

The full "fee-free days" picture across public-land agencies (published on the official Recreation.gov blog) adds a few dates for each agency:

- **U.S. Forest Service** — adds Jan 19 (MLK Day), June 6 (National Trails Day), June 13 (National Get Outdoors Day), July 4 (Independence Day), September 26 (National Public Lands Day).
- **Bureau of Land Management (BLM)** — adds July 16 (the BLM's 80th birthday).
- **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service** — adds October 11 (first Sunday of National Wildlife Refuge Week).
- **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers** — adds June 16 (USACE birthday) and September 26 (National Public Lands Day).

Official source: <https://www.recreation.gov/articles/location-spotlight/visit-your-public-lands-on-these-fee-free-days/1166>

## Who it's for

- **U.S. residents on the listed dates** — no pass, registration, or payment required for the entrance fee.
- **Anyone, any day**, at the roughly 300+ NPS sites with no entrance fee at all (e.g., national memorials, most national battlefields, many seashores).
- **Non-U.S.-residents** pay entrance fees normally (and a $100/person surcharge at 11 flagship parks in 2026) but still get great value from the annual pass — see eligibility-passes.

## Fine print to know

- Fee-free days still mean **crowds**. The free days are often the busiest days of the year in the flagship parks; plan for congestion or pick a lesser-known site.
- Timed-entry reservation requirements are **independent** of entrance fees — a fee-free day may still require a timed reservation at heavily visited parks. Always check the park's own page.
- State laws and tribal Park sites are a separate thing: fee-free days do **not** apply to state parks (see our [state parks section](state-parks.html)).
- **At least NPS days are free of "phone reservations" for entrance itself** — every listed day, every NPS unit, you just drive/walk in free.

## A "free all the time" note

Among the *major* national parks, Entrance free is the standard for most smaller units. The State of "more than 300 out of ~430" sites with free entrance year-round is the number the NPS itself publishes — this is the biggest "free parks" bucket of all, but it isn't advertised like the flagship parks are.
