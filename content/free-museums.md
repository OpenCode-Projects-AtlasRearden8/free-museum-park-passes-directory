# Free-Museum Programs

## What it is

A handful of **standing, national free-admission programs** are available year-round (or on predictable schedules), plus a patchwork of free days run by individual museums. This section covers the national ones — they're the most reliable because they come with official websites you can check in two minutes.

---

## Museums for All (SNAP/EBT — free to $5, year-round)

**What it is:** The only nationally coordinated financial-accessibility program in the entire museum field. Visitors who present a **SNAP EBT card and a photo ID** get admission of **$5.00 or less per person** at more than **1,600 participating museums**, zoos, aquariums, botanical gardens, science centers, and historic sites, in **all 50 states, D.C., and the U.S. Virgin Islands**.

**Who it's for:**

- Anyone carrying a **state-issued SNAP EBT card** — the card works in **any state**, not just the one that issued it.
- The rate covers **up to 4 people per EBT card**; some museums extend it to larger groups or WIC cardholders voluntarily (ask; not guaranteed).
- Runs **during all normal operating hours**, with no advance registration and **no limit** on how many times you use it.
- The EBT card is shown as **identification only** — you pay any small admission price with cash/card as usual.

**Check availability (official):** Museum finder and full list at <https://museums4all.org/>. The program is run by the Institute of Museum and Library Services (a federal agency) and administered by the Association of Children's Museums.

**Fine print:** Special upcharge exhibits (IMAX, limited-run shows) aren't included, though some museums include them. Museums are required to hang and post their Museums for All info, but calling ahead is never a bad idea.

## Smithsonian museums (free every day, everyone)

**What it is:** All 21 Smithsonian museums, galleries, and the National Zoo in Washington, D.C., and New York (including the National Air and Space Museum, American History, Natural History, American Indian, African American History & Culture, and the Cooper Hewitt) charge **no general admission fee for anyone, any day of the year**. The National Zoo in D.C. is also free to enter (though timed-entry passes are sometimes needed for parking or special exhibits).

**Who it's for:** Everyone. No registration, no residency test, no conditions.

**Check availability (official):** <https://www.si.edu/visit>

**Fine print:** Special exhibitions and IMAX/planetarium shows sometimes need tickets; popular museums use free timed-entry passes during peak times to control crowds. Reserve those on the official site, never a reseller.

## Free days and free hours at individual museums

**What it is:** Most major museums run some kind of standing free window: free days for residents of a state or county, free evenings, or "pay-what-you-wish" hours. Examples of the pattern (check each museum's site for the current schedule):

- **Chicago:** Illinois residents get free basic admission at the Art Institute of Chicago on certain weekdays/evenings — <https://www.artic.edu/visit>.
- **New York City:** the Metropolitan Museum of Art asks NY State residents and NY/NJ/CT students to "pay what you wish" on general admission at the main building — <https://www.metmuseum.org/visit/plan-your-visit>.
- **Science centers and children's museums** commonly run free evenings sponsored by local businesses or foundations.

**Who it's for:** Depends on the museum — often state/county residents; always check the details on the museum's own admission page.

## Blue Star Museums (military families, seasonally)

**What it is:** A summer program each year (Memorial Day through Labor Day) in which roughly 2,000 museums nationwide offer **free admission to active-duty military personnel — including Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, and Space Force — and their families** (up to five family members). Led by the National Endowment for the Arts in cooperation with Blue Star Families and the Department of Defense.

**Who it's for:** Active-duty military ID holders (Common Access Card or NES/VetRec form) plus up to five family members. Some museums extend to veterans and Gold Star families voluntarily, but active-duty eligibility is the guaranteed floor.

**Check availability (official):** <https://www.arts.gov/initiatives/blue-star-museums>

**Fine print:** Runs only during the summer season each year; special exhibits and fundraisers can be excluded.

## Bank of America "Museums on Us" (first weekend of every month)

**What it is:** Cardholders get **free general admission on the first full weekend of every month** at more than 225 museums in the program, coast to coast.

**Who it's for:** Bank of America, Merrill, or Bank of America Private Bank credit/debit card holders (one cardholder gets one free admission — not the whole family). The card is required; a photo ID matching it means the gate can spot-check.

**Check availability (official):** <https://about.bankofamerica.com/en/making-an-impact/museums-on-us>

**Fine print:** General admission only — special exhibitions often cost extra; participation varies by city.

## Honorable mentions (free by design, not by program)

- **National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C.** — free for everyone, every day: <https://www.nga.gov/>
- **Every municipal museum that never charges.** Smaller towns' city-run museums, local historical societies, and visitor centers are frequently free all year yet never show up in national "free museums" articles — the local library and city website are the best directories for these.

## Fine print common to all of these

- Free admission is almost always for **general collection** access. Blockbusters, Imax, parking, and coat checks are moneymakers everywhere.
- Timed passes: even "free" museums in D.C. and beyond sometimes use free-but-required timed-entry passes during peak periods. Reserve directly from the official museum site.
