Free Culture & Outdoors Directory

A plain-English directory of legitimately free and low-cost ways to see culture and the outdoors in the United States: library museum-pass lending programs, national-park free entrance days, state-park free days, standing free-museum programs, and the student, teacher, and military passes worth knowing about.

Every entry includes:

  • What it is — the program in one or two sentences.
  • Who it’s for — eligibility, stated honestly (including the fine print that trips people up).
  • Check availability — a link to the official source, so you can confirm details before you go.

Nothing here requires an account, sign-up at a tracker, personal information sent to us, or any payment to us. All links go to official government or organization websites. Details can change — always confirm with the official page linked in each section.

Related projects: This directory covers programs anyone (or almost anyone) can use. It is distinct from our Free Senior Discounts Directory (age-based discounts, including many museum and park deals for older adults) and our Free Read-Aloud Directory (where to find books and stories to read aloud or listen to).

The 60-second version

  • Your library card is a secret museum pass. Many large public libraries lend free museum passes good at local museums and attractions. Check your own library’s website for a “museum pass” or “attraction pass” page — this is the single highest-payoff lookup on this page.
  • SNAP/EBT household? Museums for All gives free to $5 admission for up to four people per EBT card at more than 1,600 museums, zoos, and gardens, year-round, in all 50 states.
  • Have a 4th grader? The Every Kid Outdoors pass gets your whole carload of family into every national park site, free, for a full year starting that September.
  • Active-duty military, Gold Star families, or a permanent disability? You qualify for a free America the Beautiful pass (annual or lifetime). See eligibility passes.
  • Just want a free park day? The National Park Service waves entrance fees on eight 2026 dates — see the national parks section for the full list as published by the official NPS site.

How to use this directory

  1. Pick the section that matches your situation (everyone, EBT cardholder, 4th-grader family, military family, library cardholder…).
  2. Open the official check-availability link in each section. We deliberately link to official government and program pages — not to third-party aggregators that bury the details behind ads.
  3. Confirm the current rules before you go. Free programs change dates, prices, and eligibility more often than almost anything else on the internet. A two-minute check beats a sad trip to the gate.

Honest notes

  • “Free” almost never covers everything. Campsites, parking, timed-entry reservations, special exhibitions, tours, and rentals usually still cost money even on free days. Each section says what is and isn’t waived.
  • Some programs are region-specific. Library pass lending is a local-library decision, and state park free days are set state by state — so those sections show you how to check your own area rather than one universal list.
