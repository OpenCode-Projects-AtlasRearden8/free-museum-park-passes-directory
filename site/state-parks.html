State Park Free Days and Free State Programs

What it is

There is no national list of state-park free days — each state sets its own. Some states have no entrance fees at all, some waive the annual parking/entry pass on selected days (holiday weekends, state park anniversaries, free fishing weekends), and some waive only the day-use fee while keeping camping fees.

This section teaches you the lookup once, then gives verified, high-confidence examples from states with well-documented programs so you can see the pattern.

How to check availability near you

Two steps that work in every state:

  1. Search “[your state] state parks free entrance days 2026” and click through to the state parks agency’s own domain (usually parks.<state>.gov or <state>.gov/parks).
  2. On that site, look for pages titled “Fee-Free Days,” “Free Park Days,” “Discover Pass free days” (in Washington), or “no-fee days.” Reservation-free walk-in days are usually listed on the front page of the parks agency during the season.

Other state programs (search these in the pattern above)

  • California: about 30 free-park days a year — check https://www.parks.ca.gov/ for “Free Park Days.” Note that the annual pass program and free days apply to day-use; camping always costs money.
  • Texas: free park days are announced by the Texas Parks & Wildlife Department — https://tpwd.texas.gov/state-parks/. Texas also has a free Texas State Parks Pass lending program in partnership with public libraries, which is a wonderful little-known trick: some Texas libraries loan the State Parks Pass itself.
  • New York: New York State parks charge entrance fees per vehicle; on select days (e.g., free times of Get Outdoors & Get Together Day, MLK Day-area events) they’re free — check https://parks.ny.gov/.
  • Minnesota: Minnesota state parks charge a vehicle permit; the DNR offers several free park days a year — https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/state_parks/.
  • Oregon: most Oregon state parks are free to enter (day-use fees apply at some) — https://stateparks.oregon.gov/.
  • Virginia: some Virginia State Parks have fee-free days (outdoor learning days, Public Lands Day) — https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/.
  • Missouri: many Missouri state parks are free to enter year-round — one of the friendliest systems in the country — https://mostateparks.com/.

Who it’s for

  • Everyone, with no income test. Free days are the state-park equivalent of NPS fee-free days.
  • Library cardholders in states running park-pass lending (notably Texas and some county systems elsewhere) — ask your library.
  • Fourth graders and their families — note that Every Kid Outdoors (a federal pass, see eligibility passes) is accepted at some state parks but does not generally waive state-park entry; check the state agency’s page.

Fine print to know

  • Free days waive entry/parking only. Camping, cabins, boat rentals, and guided tours cost money as usual.
  • Weekends with free days are the busiest ones; arrive early.
  • Pass lending programs (Texas libraries, some county libraries) are local decisions, not statewide — call your branch.
